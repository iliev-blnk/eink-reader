A: chin keys

58 × 132 × 12 mm

  • Narrowest and longest.
  • Buttons sit in a row under the screen, so the thumb has to reach down.
  • The PCB goes in the 22 mm chin, the battery behind the display.

B: side keys

82 × 110 × 12 mm

Recommended

  • Pages turn with one thumb.
  • The PCB sits beside the display, so the ESP32 antenna is clear of the display board's ground plane.
  • The battery goes behind the display.
  • Left-handers turn it 180° and the firmware flips the screen.
  • Widest of the three.

C: edge keys

58 × 110 × 13 mm

  • Smallest, barely bigger than the display board.
  • Edge buttons are easy to press by accident.
  • Everything stacks behind the display, so it is the thickest and the antenna sits under the ground plane.

All three have USB-C on the bottom edge, the power switch on the top edge, the microSD slot on the edge nearest the PCB, and four screws on the back.

What's real here

  • The display is modelled from the GDEY037T03 panel and WeAct board drawings: 240 × 416 px on a 47.04 × 81.54 mm active area.
  • Case sizes are estimates until the PCB is laid out.
  • Sleep and Bring-up are exactly what the firmware draws today. The chip, memory, SD and battery values on them are samples.
  • Reader and Library are mock layouts drawn with the firmware's code. They preview the text layout work, not the final engine.
  • The refresh flashes are imitated, not timed.
  • The banana is 19 cm along its outer curve.
  • Texts: Ion Creangă, Mihai Eminescu, Ioan Slavici and Fyodor Dostoevsky, public domain, from Wikisource.
